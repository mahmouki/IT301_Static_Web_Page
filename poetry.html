Poetry

After taking a poetry class for my minor, I discovered a love for writing verse about a lot of things. Most of the times the poems are not about the places themselves, but inspired by them.

Rocktown Trails

It’s Auburn at the Top

Eyes half lidded
Castin glances heavensward
The overgrown grass and leaves
Make my skin itch and tingle

Birds chirp and dogs bark
The breeze aids my hair
In its dastardly plot to blind me
I rake it back and stand up

Before me is the incline
One that thwarts me
I’m more inclined to blink
And let my leaves fall too

It’s a steep ascent,
Puddles too acidic to steep in
One of a myriad of missteps
Now my socks are damp

Born too stubborn
To simply leave it here
Born too stubborn
For my own good

One, two, three, four
Endless repetitions,
A root hooks my foot
I catch myself
My lungs refuse air
I catch my breath

With such a bleak trail,
How do I keep going?
Because once upon a time
I caught a glimpse

Of what awaited me
At the top of the hill
And decided the view
Is worth the hike.

South Main Street

An Empty Night

Oh, you are bright tonight
My star, my guiding beacon
Grant me your clarity
Eyes ever turned skyward

Your pinkie nudges mine
A ballet of our blades
My fingers curl around yours
Vines seeking sunlight
Wrapping tighter and tighter
Hoping to one day bloom

Our noses cross
Your exhale ghosts my lips
Close and soft
I can hear the blood in my ears
The wave of red across my cheeks

Swimming in the Styx,
Of your blue moon pupils
My breath halts and I ask
“How could I not look back?”

Round and round we go,
Till my head is dizzy
And my heart dazed too
Take from me my breath

Oh what a waltz
We’ve found ourselves in
Never ending marches
Quarter quarter quarter
Your reed squeaks
And so too does my bones

I blink, and it’s gone
You’re a constellation.
Out of reach again,
And never mine to touch.

Arboretum

Oshibana

The day that I wilt
They’ll find me facing west
Still warm to the touch
The deep blue lunar crests
Inked firmaments filled with teardrops

Petals scattered on the breeze
Careening in vague directions
The deer will hear me whisper
On the weak willed winds
Of tans and worthwhile sunburns

A young girl meanders
Her faint fingers pluck a sunflower
And press it dry into a book
To behold its aureate visage

She’ll never know
Why the petals were so bright
And she’ll never think
That she put the flower
in a paperback cage,
Where it’ll never see west again.
